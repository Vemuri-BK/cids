1. CONCISE LITERATURE OVERVIEW (LAST 5-7 YEARS: 2019-2026)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Over the past seven years, automated volumetric segmentation of primary breast
carcinoma in dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) has
evolved across three distinct methodological waves:

Wave 1: 3D Volumetric Encoder-Decoder CNNs (2019-2022)
The introduction of residual 3D encoder-decoders with variational autoencoder
regularization (SegResNet; Myronenko, 2019) and self-configuring biomedical
segmentation pipelines (nnU-Net; Isensee et al., 2021) established strong
voxel-level benchmarks across volumetric MRI. Open-source standardization via
MONAI (Cardoso et al., 2022) further accelerated adoption. However, clinical
studies in breast DCE-MRI quickly identified a physiological bottleneck unique
to gadolinium contrast kinetics: non-tumoral enhancing tissues—specifically
background parenchymal enhancement (BPE; van der Velden et al., 2020; Wu et al.,
2021), internal mammary and lateral thoracic blood vessels, and reactive axillary
lymph nodes—share rapid wash-in enhancement profiles with malignant lesions,
producing pervasive false-positive connected components across multi-site cohorts.

Wave 2: Promptable Vision Foundation Models in Medical Imaging (2023-2024)
Following the release of the Segment Anything Model (SAM; Kirillov et al., 2023),
medical imaging researchers developed domain-adapted 2D and 3D promptable
foundation models, notably MedSAM (Ma et al., 2024), SAM-Med3D (Wang et al.,
2023/2024), SegVol (Du et al., 2024), and Medical SAM 2 (Zhu et al., 2024). By
leveraging large-scale vision transformer (ViT) encoders conditioned on spatial
prompts (bounding boxes or clicks), these models demonstrated strong local
boundary delineation. Yet, by architectural design, promptable foundation models
require human-in-the-loop localization prompts and cannot autonomously screen
unprompted 3D clinical volumes.

Wave 3: Multi-Centre Benchmarking, Detector-Prompter Coupling, and Radiomic
        Verification (2024-2026)
The release of the 1,506-patient multi-centre MAMA-MIA benchmark (Garrucho et
al., 2025) across four clinical trials/cohorts (Duke, I-SPY 1, I-SPY 2, and NACT)
exposed how severely institutional scanner shifts (1.5T vs. 3T) and post-neoadjuvant
chemotherapy contrast blunting degrade standalone 3D segmentation networks.
Recent work has consequently shifted toward two-stage "Finder-Checker" paradigms
that couple an unprompted 3D CNN detector with a promptable foundation model or
second-stage verifier, alongside growing scrutiny of whether automated masks
preserve downstream quantitative radiomic biomarkers (Zwanenburg et al., 2020;
Golden et al., 2021).

2. KEY DEBATES & CONTRASTING FINDINGS (CONSENSUS VS. DISAGREEMENT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. Consensus in the Literature:
   1. Voxel-wise 3D CNNs Alone Are Insufficient Against BPE & Vascular Mimics:
      There is broad agreement that standard 3D U-Net/SegResNet architectures
      suffer from high false-positive component rates in breast DCE-MRI due to
      physiological BPE and thoracic vasculature (Garrucho et al., 2025; Wu et
      al., 2021).
   2. Morphological Post-Processing Rules Are Fragile:
      Heuristic connected-component pruning (e.g., keeping only the largest
      component or discarding components below 20% of the dominant volume) is
      widely recognized to fail in multifocal/multicentric breast cancer or when
      a hypervascular lymph node exceeds the volume of a small primary tumour.
   3. Segmentation Errors Corrupt Clinical Radiomic Biomarkers:
      Scholars agree that false-positive inclusions drastically distort
      volumetric and textural biomarkers (e.g., 3D tumour volume, surface-to-volume
      ratio), reducing Intraclass Correlation (ICC) and Lin's Concordance
      Correlation Coefficient (CCC) below clinical utility thresholds (Zwanenburg
      et al., 2020; Golden et al., 2021).

B. Active Debates & Contrasting Findings:
   1. Debate 1 — End-to-End Hard-Negative Decoder Fine-Tuning vs. Frozen
      Foundation Model Verification:
      * One school of thought advocates fine-tuning promptable foundation model
        decoders (via LoRA or prompt tuning) on detector-generated hard false
        positives so the decoder outputs an empty mask on non-tumours (Zhang et
        al., 2024; Hu et al., 2022).
      * Contrasting empirical evidence (including recent multi-centre DCE-MRI
        analyses) shows that training a prompt-conditioned mask decoder on hard
        negatives induces a global threshold shift ("calibration collapse"),
        sacrificing true-tumour sensitivity without improving ranking ROC-AUC.
        This supports keeping the foundation model frozen as an evidence provider
        and delegating candidate rejection to an external classifier.
   2. Debate 2 — Deep Latent Representations vs. Handcrafted 3D Morphological
      and Local-Contrast Radiomics:
      * Foundation-model proponents argue that 3D ViT embeddings capture all
        necessary semantic context (Ma et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2023).
      * Conversely, biophysical imaging researchers contend that downsampled ViT
        patch tokens lose explicit 3D differential geometry (e.g., tubular vessel
        elongation, convex-hull solidity) and local peritumoral contrast-to-noise
        kinetics (Kuhl, 2007; Zwanenburg et al., 2020), making handcrafted 3D
        shape and local contrast clues competitive with—or complementary to—deep
        foundation embeddings.

4. FOUR RECURRING THEMES / CLUSTERS IN THE LITERATURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Conceptual Cluster — Macro Candidate Verification vs. Micro Boundary Refinement:
   Studies increasingly recognize that in large field-of-view 3D breast MRI,
   volumetric overlap errors are dominated by macro region selection (false-positive
   vascular/lymphatic/BPE components vs. multifocal satellites) rather than
   sub-millimetre boundary smoothing (Setio et al., 2017; Garrucho et al., 2025).

2. Methodological Cluster — Prompt Automation and Non-Parametric Memory Retrieval:
   Bridging interactive 3D foundation models (SAM-Med3D, MedSAM) into autonomous
   clinical workflows requires replacing manual prompts with detector-derived
   candidate boxes, coupled with non-parametric exemplar retrieval (k-nearest
   neighbour similarity and linear probing over frozen foundation embeddings)
   cross-fitted by patient to prevent data leakage (Wang et al., 2023; Lewis et
   al., 2020).

3. Biophysical & Radiomic Cluster — Self-Normalized 3D Morphology and Local
   Contrast Kinetics:
   Because multi-centre MRI archives often lack standardized quantitative T1
   mapping and exhibit variable fields of view, effective candidate verification
   relies on 3D shape descriptors (elongation, flatness, sphericity, solidity,
   surface-to-volume ratio; Zwanenburg et al., 2020), local peritumoral ring
   contrast-to-noise ratios (Kuhl, 2007), distance to the patient's most
   confident anchor candidate, and within-patient relative normalization.

4. Empirical & Translational Cluster — Multi-Cohort Generalization and
   Downstream Biomarker Agreement:
   Modern evaluation standards demand testing across heterogeneous clinical
   collections (Leave-One-Hospital-Out cross-validation across DUKE, ISPY1,
   ISPY2, and NACT) and verifying that segmentation improvements translate into
   clinical volumetric agreement via ICC(2,1) (absolute agreement), ICC(3,1)
   (consistency), and Lin's CCC (Shrout & Fleiss, 1979; Lin, 1989).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CLEAR RESEARCH GAPS & UNDEREXPLORED DIMENSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Gap 1 (The Prompting & Hard-Negative Dilemma): Promptable 3D foundation models
  (SAM-Med3D) cannot self-localize tumours, yet fine-tuning their mask decoders
  directly on detector false positives degrades true-tumour sensitivity without
  improving candidate ranking AUC.
* Gap 2 (Lack of Rigorous Comparison Between Foundation-Model Memory and
  Handcrafted 3D Radiomic Clues): Few studies systematically test whether a 3D
  foundation model is strictly necessary for candidate verification, or whether
  handcrafted 3D shape, local ring contrast, anchor distance, and within-patient
  relative clues achieve comparable or superior false-positive rejection.
* Gap 3 (Multi-Hospital Calibration Drift): Raw detector probabilities and
  foundation-model agreement scores shift across scanners and protocols; the
  role of within-patient relative normalization in enabling zero-shot
  Leave-One-Hospital-Out (LOHO) generalization remains underexplored.
* Gap 4 (Unverified Volumetric Biomarker Concordance): Segmentation benchmarks
  rarely quantify whether automated candidate verification significantly reduces
  absolute tumour volume error (mL) and improves ICC(2,1) / Lin's CCC against
  manual radiologist delineations.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. POSITIONING PARAGRAPH FOR YOUR STUDY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To address these gaps, the present study introduces a fully automatic
Finder-Checker-Jury framework evaluated on 1,506 multi-centre breast DCE-MRI
examinations from the MAMA-MIA benchmark (Garrucho et al., 2025). Rather than
destructively fine-tuning the foundation model's decoder on hard negatives—which
we empirically show collapses sensitivity without improving discrimination—our
framework pairs an unprompted 3D SegResNet candidate Finder with a box-prompted
SAM-Med3D-turbo Checker and adjudicates candidates through a patient-cross-fitted
Memory and Radiomic Jury (`jury13_rad_rel`). By integrating 13 Finder, Checker,
and frozen foundation-embedding memory cues with 15 handcrafted 3D morphological,
peritumoral ring-contrast, texture, and anchor-distance clues alongside 5
within-patient relative clues, the proposed jury raises candidate ranking AUC
from 0.917 (learned Finder-Checker baseline) and 0.942 (SAM memory jury) to
0.968 on the 306-patient official test set (Validation Dice: 0.787, Test Dice:
0.743, median HD95: 3.16 mm), while demonstrating that a lightweight Finder +
Radiomics verifier (`finder_rad`, 0.962 Test AUC) already provides a fast,
foundation-free alternative. Crucially, Leave-One-Hospital-Out validation
confirms zero-shot generalization across all four clinical cohorts, and
downstream biomarker analysis proves that our pipeline reduces mean absolute
tumour volume error by 28.3% compared to morphological post-processing (11.74 mL
to 8.42 mL), raising absolute volumetric agreement (`ICC(2,1)` and `Lin's CCC`)
from 0.825 to 0.879.
