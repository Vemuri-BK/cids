TITLE:
CIDS: A Fully Automatic Finder–Checker Framework with Learned Foundation Model
Verification and Frequency-Guided Refinement for 3D Breast Tumour DCE-MRI Segmentation

AUTHORS:
Bharath Kumar Vemuri, et al.


================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================
Volumetric primary tumour segmentation in breast dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic
resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) underpins quantitative staging, phenotypic profiling,
and neoadjuvant chemotherapy response assessment. However, fully automatic 3D
segmentation is severely impeded less by boundary delineation errors than by pervasive
false-positive enhancement: tortuous blood vessels, benign hypervascularized axillary
lymph nodes, and physiological background parenchymal enhancement (BPE) regularly
trick voxel-wise networks into predicting non-tumoral enhancing tissue as carcinoma.
In the 1,506-case multi-centre MAMA-MIA benchmark, 66% of official test examinations
contain at least one false-positive connected component under a competitive SegResNet
baseline. Promptable 3D medical foundation models such as SAM-Med3D yield high
segmentation fidelity when supplied with human-in-the-loop oracle prompts (0.708 Dice
on test data), but they cannot localize targets autonomously. Conversely, fine-tuning
the foundation model decoder on hard false positives degrades true-tumour sensitivity
from 99.7% to 65.0% without improving overall discriminability (AUC drops from 0.80 to
0.77).

To resolve this dilemma, we propose CIDS (Clinical Instruction Dual Supervision /
Candidate Inspection and Decision Supervision), a fully automatic hierarchical
framework that decouples macro candidate selection from micro boundary refinement.
CIDS employs a 3D segmentation network (SegResNet) as an unprompted candidate Finder,
an adapted SAM-Med3D-turbo model as a local Checker operating on automatic bounding
boxes trained with realistic and negative prompts, and a non-linear Learned Verifier.
The verifier combines gradient-boosted trees over 11 geometric, detector-confidence,
and checker-response cues with non-parametric memory retrieval (kNN density and linear
probe) over 768-dimensional frozen foundation model embeddings cross-fitted by patient.
To surpass the theoretical candidate-selection ceiling (oracle Dice 0.7901), a
boundary-confined 3D Haar wavelet refiner conditions high-frequency directional subbands
on low-frequency context to adjust boundaries within an 8 mm signed-distance envelope.

Evaluated on the 306-case official multi-centre test set of MAMA-MIA across four
clinical cohorts (Duke, I-SPY 1, I-SPY 2, NACT), learned verification improves the mean
Dice from 0.684 (segmentation network with morphological filtering) and 0.699 (rule-based
agreement filtering) to 0.7495 (p < 10^-4), halves the median 95th-percentile Hausdorff
distance from 7.3 mm to 3.3 mm, raises the normalised surface Dice (NSD at 2 mm) from
0.716 to 0.799, and reduces missed tumours from 14 to 10. In the enhancement-dominated
Duke cohort, test Dice surges from 0.544 to 0.701 (+0.157 gain). The candidate verifier
separates true from false lesions with an AUC of 0.938. Our findings establish that
frozen foundation models serve as superior evidence providers whose soft response can
be unlocked via lightweight learned verification without destructive fine-tuning.

SECTION I: INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) is the most sensitive
clinical imaging modality for the detection, local staging, and phenotypic
characterization of breast carcinoma [1], [2]. In patients undergoing neoadjuvant
chemotherapy (NAC), volumetric changes in primary tumour burden and kinetics between
pre-treatment and mid-treatment MRI scans serve as potent imaging biomarkers for
predicting pathologic complete response (pCR) and long-term recurrence-free survival
[3], [4]. Furthermore, quantitative 3D radiomic profiling requires precise volumetric
delineations of the primary lesion [5]. In routine clinical practice, however, manual
slice-by-slice 3D contouring of irregular, multilocular, or non-mass enhancing lesions
across large volumetric DCE-MRI series is exceedingly time-consuming, labor-intensive,
and plagued by high inter-observer variability [6]. Consequently, robust and fully
autonomous 3D segmentation tools are of paramount clinical and translational importance.

Over the past decade, 3D convolutional neural networks (CNNs) and vision transformers,
exemplified by nnU-Net [7] and SegResNet [8], have achieved benchmark-setting overlap
accuracy across many biomedical segmentation domains. Nevertheless, when applied to
volumetric breast DCE-MRI, standard voxel-wise encoder-decoder networks encounter a
formidable, modality-specific failure mode: pervasive false-positive enhancement.
Malignant breast lesions induce contrast uptake through tumour-associated angiogenesis
and hyperpermeable microvasculature. However, normal fibroglandular parenchyma also
enhances to varying degrees after intravenous gadolinium administration—a physiological
phenomenon designated as background parenchymal enhancement (BPE) [9], [10]. Moreover,
hypervascularized axillary lymph nodes and tortuous blood vessels (such as the internal
thoracic, lateral thoracic, and intercostal vascular bundles) exhibit intense, rapid
contrast uptake curves that closely mirror primary breast carcinoma [11]. In large
heterogeneous datasets, such as the 1,506-patient multi-centre MAMA-MIA benchmark [12],
our analysis reveals that 66% of official test examinations contain at least one
high-confidence false-positive connected component produced by a competitive 3D
SegResNet baseline. Because volumetric overlap metrics (e.g., the Dice similarity
coefficient) severely penalize false-positive voxels, deciding *which* candidate
regions represent true malignancy is just as critical to clinical utility as refining
tumor boundaries.

To eliminate false-positive components, standard segmentation pipelines typically resort
to morphological post-processing heuristics, such as discarding all components except the
single largest one, or pruning components smaller than a fixed percentage (e.g., 20%)
of the dominant lesion [12], [13]. These heuristics fail catastrophically in multifocal
and multicentric carcinomas—where biologically aggressive satellite lesions are
inadvertently erased—and in cases where an intensely enhancing reactive lymph node
exceeds the volume of a small primary carcinoma. While multi-parametric imaging
combining diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC)
maps can theoretically distinguish cellular restriction from benign BPE [14], functional
DWI sequences suffer from severe susceptibility artifacts, low signal-to-noise ratio,
and spatial distortions that hinder automated registration with high-resolution DCE-MRI.
Thus, resolving false-positive enhancement directly within volumetric DCE-MRI remains an
unsolved operational bottleneck.

In parallel, the advent of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) [15] and its volumetric
medical adaptations, such as SAM-Med3D [16], MedSAM [17], and SegVol [18], has
demonstrated the unprecedented zero-shot boundary delineation capabilities of large-scale
foundation models. When supplied with expert human-in-the-loop prompts (such as a 3D
bounding box derived from ground-truth annotations), our fine-tuned SAM-Med3D-turbo
achieves an impressive 0.708 Dice on the MAMA-MIA test set, comfortably outperforming
unprompted SegResNet (0.684). However, promptable foundation models present a fundamental
clinical dilemma: they are strictly interactive engines that possess no inherent
autonomous detection capacity. They cannot segment an examination without an initial
spatial prompt.

A seemingly intuitive solution is to couple an automatic detector with a foundation
model, fine-tuning the foundation model's mask decoder via hard-negative mining to
output an empty mask whenever prompted by a detector false alarm. However, as our
systematic investigation demonstrates, this adaptation strategy triggers a severe
calibration collapse: the foundation model trades true-positive sensitivity for
false-positive rejection, retaining only 65.0% of genuine tumours while its discriminative
capacity (measured by the area under the ROC curve, AUC) drops from 0.80 to 0.77.
Fine-tuning the mask decoder on negative prompts forces the model to shift its global
decision threshold rather than learning finer feature discrimination.

In this work, we propose CIDS (Candidate Inspection and Decision Supervision), a
hierarchical, fully automatic framework that overcomes these limitations by decoupling
the macro decision of candidate verification from the micro decision of boundary
refinement. The pipeline operates in three synergistic stages:
1. Candidate Proposal (The Finder): A 3D SegResNet operating without prompts generates
   multi-component lesion candidates.
2. Foundation Model Inspection (The Checker): Each candidate bounding box is forwarded
   as a prompt to SAM-Med3D-turbo, which has been adapted with low-rank adapters (LoRA)
   and trained on realistic, perturbed, and negative boxes to evaluate each candidate.
3. Learned Candidate Verification (The Verifier): Rather than fine-tuning the checker's
   decoder or relying on fragile agreement thresholds (e.g., Dice > 0.7), we treat the
   frozen foundation model as an objective evidence provider. A non-linear
   gradient-boosted decision tree ensemble [19] evaluates 11 multidimensional cues
   spanning candidate geometry, finder probability distributions, and foundation model
   soft-volume responses. To exploit population-level visual memories, we augment this
   classifier with non-parametric retrieval (kNN density and linear probing) over
   768-dimensional frozen foundation model embeddings cross-fitted by patient, driving
   test Dice to 0.7495.
4. Frequency Boundary Refinement: Because macro candidate selection is mathematically
   bounded by a combinatorial ceiling (oracle Dice 0.7901 in MAMA-MIA), pushing beyond
   this limit necessitates sub-voxel boundary correction. We introduce a dedicated 3D
   Haar wavelet refiner [20] that isolates directional high-frequency subbands from
   low-frequency context within an 8 mm boundary band, evaluated against an exact
   capacity-matched spatial convolution twin to rigorously benchmark the wavelet
   inductive bias.

The primary contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
- We formulate a fully automatic, prompt-free finder-checker-verifier architecture for
  volumetric breast DCE-MRI tumour segmentation that integrates the localization power
  of 3D CNNs with the rich representational fidelity of 3D foundation models without
  requiring human prompts.
- We present a learned candidate verification paradigm combining gradient-boosted trees
  with non-parametric memory retrieval over frozen foundation model embeddings, raising
  the official MAMA-MIA test Dice from 0.684 to 0.7495 (p < 10^-4), halving the median
  HD95 from 7.3 mm to 3.3 mm, and achieving a candidate discrimination AUC of 0.938.
- We provide empirical and theoretical evidence showing that retraining foundation model
  decoders on hard negatives degrades sensitivity without improving discrimination,
  proving that frozen foundation models act as superior feature providers when paired
  with an external learned decision layer.
- We design a boundary-confined 3D Haar wavelet refiner that operates within an 8 mm
  signed-distance margin around the verified mask, strictly benchmarked against an
  identical-capacity spatial twin to investigate frequency-domain boundary recovery.
- We conduct extensive multi-centre validation across 1,506 examinations from four
  heterogeneous cohorts (Duke, I-SPY 1, I-SPY 2, NACT), demonstrating dramatic specificity
  gains in heavily enhancing imaging cohorts (Duke Dice: 0.544 -> 0.701) and publishing
  a standalone, fully deployable inference pipeline.
