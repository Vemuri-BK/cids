1. THE CORE RESEARCH PROBLEM AND WHAT THIS WORK SOLVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. The Existing Problem in the Field:
Dynamic contrast-enhanced magnetic resonance imaging (DCE-MRI) is the clinical gold
standard for breast cancer staging, phenotypic subtyping, and monitoring response
to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC). However, fully automatic 3D primary tumor
segmentation faces a fundamental physiological and computational hurdle:
1. False-Positive Enhancement Dominance: Normal breast physiology causes contrast
   enhancement in non-tumoral structures, notably background parenchymal enhancement
   (BPE), tortuous blood vessels (internal/lateral thoracic vessels), and reactive
   axillary lymph nodes. Standard 3D voxel-level convolutional and transformer
   segmenters (e.g., SegResNet, nnU-Net) share high false-positive rates on these
   structures. In the multi-centre MAMA-MIA benchmark (1,506 examinations across 4
   clinical cohorts), 66% of test volumes contain at least one false-positive
   connected component that degrades the volume-overlap Dice score.
2. The Prompting Dilemma of Foundation Models: Volumetric promptable foundation models
   (e.g., SAM-Med3D, MedSAM) achieve remarkable segmentation accuracy when supplied
   with human-in-the-loop expert bounding boxes or clicks. However, they are inherently
   interactive and lack semantic self-localization—they cannot operate autonomously
   without an external prompter.
3. The Adaptation Trap: When researchers attempt to train or fine-tune foundation
   model decoders directly on hard false positives (teaching the model to reject
   non-tumor candidates with empty masks), the network merely shifts its operating
   point—sacrificing tumor sensitivity (retaining only ~65% of true tumors) without
   improving overall discriminative capability (AUC stagnates or drops).
4. The Candidate Selection Ceiling: Rule-based heuristics (e.g., keeping candidates
   where two models agree with Dice > 0.7) discard atypical or irregularly shaped
   true tumors. Furthermore, even an optimal selection of candidates (the oracle
   subset) is mathematically capped at a Dice ceiling (~0.790 in MAMA-MIA) because
   it cannot refine sub-voxel or high-frequency boundary discrepancies.

B. What This Work Proposes and Solves:
This work introduces a hierarchical, fully automatic framework (CIDS) that decouples
the macro decision of *which* regions to keep from the micro decision of *where*
boundaries lie:
1. Finder-Checker-Verifier Hierarchy: A 3D segmentation network (SegResNet) acts as
   an unprompted candidate Finder; an adapted 3D foundation model (SAM-Med3D-turbo)
   acts as a candidate Checker using automatically derived bounding boxes trained
   with realistic, perturbed, and negative boxes; and a non-linear Learned Verifier
   makes the final decision.
2. Foundation Models as Evidence Providers: We show that the frozen foundation
   model already possesses high candidate discrimination in its soft continuous
   predictions and latent embeddings (AUC ~0.82–0.92). Rather than disrupting the
   decoder weights with hard-negative fine-tuning, a lightweight gradient-boosted
   decision layer over candidate-level cues exploits this latent evidence without
   harming true-positive sensitivity.
3. Memory-Augmented Verification: Incorporating a non-parametric memory bank of
   frozen foundation model descriptors with patient-grouped cross-fitting (kNN and
   linear probe) enables the verifier to reach a state-of-the-art Test Dice of
   0.7495 (reducing median HD95 from 7.3 mm to 3.3 mm).
4. Frequency Boundary Refinement: To overcome the candidate selection ceiling,
   we introduce a boundary-confined 3D Haar wavelet refiner that isolates directional
   high-frequency subbands from low-frequency context, evaluated against an
   exact capacity-matched spatial twin to provide an unambiguous ablation of
   wavelet representations in 3D medical boundary correction.

2. RELATED WORK (MANUSCRIPT SECTION DRAFT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A. 3D Deep Segmentation Networks in Breast DCE-MRI and False-Positive Enhancement
Volumetric encoder-decoder architectures, most notably the self-configuring nnU-Net
[1] and residual variants such as SegResNet [2], have established high baseline
performance across diverse biomedical imaging domains. In breast DCE-MRI, however,
voxel-wise segmentation networks encounter a distinct failure mode driven by contrast
kinetics [3, 4]. Intravenously administered gadolinium-based contrast agents perfuse
not only malignant lesions exhibiting neoangiogenesis and increased vascular
permeability, but also normal fibroglandular tissue—a phenomenon quantified as
background parenchymal enhancement (BPE) [5, 6]. Furthermore, physiological vascular
structures (such as the internal mammary and lateral thoracic vessels) and benign
hypervascularized axillary lymph nodes display rapid early enhancement curves that
closely mirror primary breast carcinoma [7].

In large multi-center clinical studies, such as the 1,506-patient MAMA-MIA benchmark
aggregated from Duke, I-SPY 1, I-SPY 2, and NACT clinical cohorts [8], voxel-level
networks regularly predict multiple high-confidence connected components outside the
true tumor volume. Conventional remedies rely on heuristic connected-component
filtering, such as retaining only the single largest connected component or pruning
components smaller than a fixed relative volume threshold (e.g., rel20) [8, 9].
These morphological heuristics fail systematically in multifocal or multicentric
breast carcinomas (where true satellite lesions are eliminated) or when prominent
axillary lymph nodes exceed the volume of a small primary tumor. While multi-parametric
MRI incorporating diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) and apparent diffusion coefficient
(ADC) maps has been explored to differentiate cellular restriction from benign BPE
[10, 11], non-contrast functional sequences suffer from low spatial resolution,
severe geometric distortion, and frequent spatial misalignment with high-resolution
T1-weighted DCE-MRI. Consequently, effective false-positive rejection directly within
volumetric DCE-MRI remains a critical clinical requirement.


B. Promptable Foundation Models in Medical Imaging
The introduction of the Segment Anything Model (SAM) [12] marked a major paradigm
shift toward prompt-driven, generalist foundation models for computer vision. SAM's
heavy vision transformer (ViT) image encoder produces rich visual representations,
while a lightweight promptable decoder enables zero-shot segmentation conditioned on
interactive user prompts (points, bounding boxes, or coarse masks). To transfer these
capabilities to clinical settings, domain-adapted medical variants have emerged,
including MedSAM [13], SAM-Med2D [14], and 3D volumetric extensions such as
SAM-Med3D [15], SegVol [16], and Medical SAM 2 [17].

Despite their high boundary delineation fidelity, promptable medical foundation
models exhibit two major limitations when deployed in clinical workflows:
1. Absence of Fully Autonomous Execution: By construction, foundation models like
   SAM-Med3D-turbo [15] cannot localize targets without external prompts. Under an
   oracle prompt setting (e.g., ground-truth bounding boxes derived from manual expert
   contours), fine-tuned SAM-Med3D achieves excellent accuracy. In clinical practice,
   however, providing manual bounding boxes on every 3D volumetric slice defeats the
   purpose of automated triage and radiomic analysis.
2. The Failure of Hard-Negative Fine-Tuning: Several works have attempted to automate
   promptable models by generating candidate prompts via an auxiliary detector and
   fine-tuning the foundation model decoder to output an empty mask when prompted by a
   false-positive region (hard-negative mining) [18, 19]. As our experimental analysis
   demonstrates, modifying the foundation model's mask decoder weights on hard false
   positives destabilizes the learned prompt conditioning: the model shifts its
   internal decision threshold, resulting in a severe drop in true-positive sensitivity
   without improving the area under the ROC curve (AUC). Our work addresses this by
   preserving the frozen foundation model as an objective evidence provider and
   shifting the discriminative burden to a dedicated verification layer.


C. Candidate Verification, Two-Stage Architectures, and Memory Augmentation
Two-stage detection and verification pipelines have a rich history in computer-aided
detection (CADe), particularly in screening mammography and CT pulmonary nodule
detection [20, 21]. In standard false-positive reduction (FPR) frameworks, such as
those popularized in the LUNA16 challenge [22], a candidate generator operates at
high sensitivity to retrieve candidate lesions, followed by a separate 3D CNN
classifier that discards non-nodular false alarms. In volumetric semantic segmentation,
however, candidate-level verification is rarely formalized; most methods resort to
voxel-wise ensembling or rigid heuristic rules, such as discarding regions where two
models disagree below a fixed Dice threshold (e.g., agreement < 0.7) [23].

Such hard rules discard non-mass enhancing lesions and atypical tumor shapes where
the foundation model segments more conservatively or generously than the initial
detector. In this work, we formalize candidate verification as a structured machine
learning problem: rather than relying on one arbitrary threshold, a gradient-boosted
decision tree ensemble [24] is trained over a multi-dimensional feature space
encompassing candidate geometry, detector confidence distributions, and foundation
model soft response metrics.

Furthermore, inspired by recent advancements in non-parametric retrieval-augmented
learning and cache-augmented generation [25, 26], we augment this verifier with a
persistent memory bank of frozen foundation model embeddings. In contrast to pure
parametric classification, non-parametric memory retrieval (via k-nearest neighbor
density estimation and linear probing) provides instance-level exemplar comparison
against verified historical training lesions. To strictly prevent data leakage, we
employ patient-grouped cross-fitting, ensuring that training candidates are evaluated
only against memory elements from independent patients.


D. Frequency-Domain Representations and Boundary Refinement
Even with optimal candidate selection, volumetric segmentation accuracy is bounded
by a mathematical ceiling: selecting the perfect subset of candidates cannot correct
sub-voxel misalignments, irregular spiculation, or blurred invasive margins.
In medical imaging, tumor boundaries frequently inhabit high-frequency spatial subbands
that are degraded by standard convolutional pooling and interpolation operations [27].

To address this, multiresolution wavelet analysis—rooted in the discrete wavelet
transform (DWT) pioneered by Mallat [28]—has gained renewed prominence in deep learning.
Wavelet Multi-scale Region-Enhanced Networks (WMREN) [29] demonstrate that combining
Haar wavelet subbands with spatial residual branches substantially improves boundary
sharpness in multi-organ CT. Similarly, FreqFusion [30] utilizes adaptive low-pass
filtering for regional semantic consistency alongside high-pass detail enhancement for
dense prediction boundaries. WaveRNet [31] integrates wavelet representations into SAM
for retinal vessel segmentation, while uncertainty-driven boundary refinement networks
such as UDBRNet [32] demonstrate that learning residual logit corrections around an
existing coarse prediction preserves macro topology while sharpening local margins.

Our work synthesizes these principles into a dedicated 3D boundary refiner. Unlike
prior architectures that feed wavelet features into a global segmentation network from
scratch, our refiner operates strictly as a residual modifier around a frozen,
verified mask M0:
    z_final = z0 + allow * sigmoid(gate) * delta
By initializing delta to zero and confining adjustments to a narrow signed-distance
band (|SDF| <= 8 mm), the network is guaranteed to maintain identity at initialization
and cannot hallucinate spurious disconnected lesions. Crucially, to isolate the
empirical benefit of frequency-domain representation, we benchmark the 3D Haar wavelet
decomposition against an exact capacity-matched spatial convolution twin, establishing
a controlled ablation of multiresolution wavelet representations in 3D DCE-MRI.
